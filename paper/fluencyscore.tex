\begin{abstract}
LLM-based machine translation evaluation has advanced rapidly since
\cite{kocmi-federmann-2023a} showed that zero-shot prompting of GPT models
reaches state-of-the-art system-level agreement with MQM ratings, and that the
capability emerges only from GPT-3.5 upward.
Later work extended the approach in several directions: MQM-style error
annotation with PaLM-2, with quality dependent on model scale
(AutoMQM; \citealp{fernandes-etal-2023-automqm}); few-shot GPT-4 prompting,
ranked among the strongest submissions at WMT23
(GEMBA-MQM; \citealp{kocmi-federmann-2023b,freitag-etal-2023-wmt});
reasoning-based prompting \cite{lu-etal-2024-error}; and repeated sampling, with
GEMBA-MQM~V2 aggregating ten GPT-4.1-mini judgments per segment to rank first on
the WMT24 MQM test sets \cite{junczys-dowmunt-2025-gemba-v2}.
The official WMT25 metrics likewise run on a GPT-4.1 backbone.

At the same time, several results indicate that further correlation gains along
this axis are limited.
Judge accuracy does not consistently track model capability
\cite{tan-etal-2025-judgebench}; human preference data measurably favors fluent
but incorrect responses \cite{sharma-etal-2024-sycophancy,openai-2025-sycophancy};
and the design of the scoring scheme itself shapes model behavior
\cite{kalai-etal-2025-hallucinate}.
Part of the headroom above current metrics is therefore annotation noise rather
than unmodeled quality.

\FS{} agrees with expert rankings on 72\% of segment pairs on WMT25 ESA and on
78\% on WMT22 MQM, ahead of every released baseline on identical segments
(MetricX-24-Hybrid, XCOMET, CometKiwi, chrF++; all $p{<}0.001$), and its pooled
segment-level Spearman of $0.61$--$0.62$ is at or above published
inter-annotator agreement for these protocols
($0.41$--$0.58$; \citealp{freitag-etal-2021-experts}).
To attribute these numbers correctly, we compare against the strongest available
baseline: GEMBA-ESA, the family used for the official WMT25 scores, re-run on the
same Gemini backbone, the same segments, and the same fair mask.
On this matched setup the two frameworks are statistically equivalent in
correlation ($\Delta\rho{=}{+}0.007$, CI covering zero); of the ${+}0.065$ gap over
the official leaderboard score, ${+}0.058$ is contributed by the backbone.
Variation across language pairs is roughly twenty times larger than any effect of
prompt design.

Our hypotheses for the structured framework therefore concern its outputs rather
than its score, and the largest confirmed return is diagnostic.
For every segment, \FS{} produces a complete error record: an issue type from a
fixed taxonomy, a severity level, the exact problem phrase, and a suggested fix.
In addition, the structured variant is substantially more stable under a change of
judge, varying within a $0.022$ Spearman range across four backbones against
$0.128$ for a flat single-score variant of the same rubric, and its per-pair
behavior is predictable from typological distance.
Further framework gains (calibration, sub-dimension weighting, language-pair
routing) remain hypotheses under active test.

The relevant question for LLM-based evaluation is thus shifting: well-prompted
judges already match human judgment up to annotation noise, so the value of an
evaluation lies in what it returns at that level of accuracy, and in whether those
returns persist when the underlying model changes.
\FS{} is designed for this setting.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
This section motivates treating fluency as a distinct evaluation axis and states the contributions of the paper.
\subsection{Problem and motivation}
Automatic MT metrics such as COMET, GEMBA, and chrF conflate several quality
dimensions and do not isolate fluency as a distinct axis.
Fluency covers idiomatic phrasing, collocational fit, discourse coherence,
pragmatic appropriateness, and freedom from calque; a single scalar rarely
exposes where a translation fails on these grounds.

WMT25 ESA and historical WMT MQM benchmarks provide independent human judgments
at the segment level \cite{kocmi-etal-2024-esa,kocmi-etal-2025-wmt25}.
We use a fair comparison protocol (same segments, no imputation, bootstrap CIs)
and complement aggregate correlation with per-segment error analysis.

\subsection{Contributions}
\begin{itemize}
\item A structured, rubric-based LLM-judge (\FS{}) with five weighted fluency
  sub-dimensions and a double-pass scoring procedure, plus a monotone
  calibration (\FSCal{}) for reporting.
\item A fair-comparison evaluation against official WMT25 metrics and against
  GEMBA-MQM / FluencyScore-MQM on WMT22--24, with bootstrap 95\% CIs and paired
  bootstrap tests; matched-backbone contrasts are primary.
\item A backbone ablation and a typological analysis linking syntactic distance
  to \FS{}'s advantage.
\item A segment-level error analysis with an issue-type taxonomy, headline
  failure modes, and a variance-collapse study of the MQM-style variant.
\end{itemize}

\section{\FS{} and \FSCal{}}
\textbf{Structured \FS{}:} the judge fills five sub-dimension scores and an
error record; we combine the five scores into one scalar aggregate.\\
\textbf{Flat variant:} the same backbone is asked for a single overall fluency
score with no rubric and no error record (our FluencyScore-MQM-flat condition).\\
\textbf{FluencyScore-MQM:} the same rubric as structured \FS{}, but scored on a
negative MQM-style scale (start high, subtract severity penalties).

\FS{} scores five fluency sub-dimensions---idiomatic, collocational, discourse,
pragmatic, and calque---and aggregates them with fixed weights into a single
value on a 1--10 scale.
The sub-dimension weights and the full judge prompt are proprietary
(Section~\ref{sec:repro}; Appendix~\ref{app:schema}).

\FSCal{} is a fixed piecewise-linear, non-decreasing map of the raw aggregate onto
a reporting scale that is easier to compare with human ESA/MQM numbers.
Because the map is non-decreasing, rank correlations are unchanged up to ties.
Parameters were fixed during development and are not fitted on any evaluation
cohort in this paper.

\subsection{Output schema, taxonomy, and scoring protocol}
\textbf{Reference-free by design.}
The judge sees the source and the translation only---no reference
(product use case: online QE / WMT AutoEval Task~3).
A reference-conditioned ablation remains future work.

For each segment the judge returns:
(i)~five sub-dimension scores on a 1--10 scale;
(ii)~the scalar aggregate (fixed weighted sum);
(iii)~one main \texttt{issue\_type} from
\{\texttt{calque}, \texttt{register\_mismatch}, \texttt{false\_friend},
\texttt{idiom\_literal}, \texttt{collocation}, \texttt{discourse\_marker},
\texttt{none}\};
(iv)~severity; (v)~problem phrase; (vi)~suggested fix;
(vii)~confidence fields (\texttt{low\_confidence}, \texttt{max\_score\_diff}).

\textbf{Double-pass.}
The judge scores each segment twice independently; we average the two aggregates.
A single-pass control shows the second call adds nothing measurable
($\Delta\rho{=}{+}0.007$, CI $[{-}0.011,{+}0.026]$).

Discourse contributes near-zero to rank correlation on our masks; we keep it as
a diagnostic channel. Weights stay fixed.

\subsection{Fair-comparison protocol}
\label{sec:fair}
\textbf{Language pairs (WMT25 fair subset).}
EN$\to$ar\_EG, bho\_IN, cs\_CZ, et\_EE, is\_IS, it\_IT, ja\_JP, mas\_KE,
ru\_RU, sr\_Cyrl\_RS, uk\_UA, zh\_CN (12 pairs; per-pair $n$ in Table~\ref{tab:1c}).

\textbf{WMT25.}
We join official automatic scores with \FS{} on aligned segments.
Starting from 4,560 scored rows, the primary fair mask keeps a row only if human
ESA and all of \{\FS{}, GEMBA-ESA, MetricX-24, XCOMET, CometKiwi\} are non-null
$\rightarrow n{=}3{,}526$ (retention 66.3\%--90.0\% by pair).
chrF++ is not part of this mask (missing for EN$\to$it\_IT); we report chrF++ on
its own subset ($n{=}4{,}200$).

\textbf{MQM.}
Join on (\texttt{year}, \texttt{pair}, \texttt{seg\_id}, \texttt{system\_id});
keep segments where every compared metric is non-null.

\textbf{Surface baseline on WMT22--24 (Exp.~3).}
We recompute chrF++ with sacrebleu (\texttt{CHRF}, \texttt{word\_order=2}) using
\texttt{mt-metrics-eval-v2} \texttt{refA} aligned by exact source-text match.
Result: Spearman $\rho{=}0.290$, PA${=}0.626$, $n{=}2{,}553$
(83 fair rows lack an exact source match and are omitted from the chrF++ column
only). This replaces the previous $n{=}818$ / WMT22-keys-only figure
($\rho{=}0.351$, PA${=}0.653$).

\textbf{Agreement statistics.}
Spearman $\rho$ (primary), Kendall $\tau$, and pairwise accuracy
PA${=}(\tau{+}1)/2$.
Headline $\rho$/$\tau$/PA are pooled unless a table says otherwise.
Table~\ref{tab:1c} also reports per-pair $\rho$ and a macro-average.

\textbf{Uncertainty.}
Bootstrap 95\% intervals by segment resampling; paired bootstrap for metric
differences.

\subsection{Baseline validation and pre-registration}
\label{sec:prereg}
Where an official score exists, our copy of a metric must match it before we
trust the copy.
For GEMBA-ESA the bar was Spearman $0.85$ against official GEMBA-ESA--GPT-4.1 on
a stratified 400-segment subset.
Our copy reached only $0.784$; we never replace an official GEMBA-ESA cell with
our copy.
Our copy appears only where no official score exists (Gemini backbone), and
captions mark those rows.

For GEMBA-MQM, no official scores exist for our MQM years.
We treat MQM cohorts as an internal ablation of \FS{} variants, with one
reference-based external anchor (WMT22 GPT-4 release).
The decisive WMT25 contrast was pre-registered (mask, statistics, outcome
meanings) before the run finished.

\subsection{Method}
\label{sec:ea-method}
Fair subsets: WMT25 ESA $n{=}3{,}526$; MQM disagreement slice $n{=}824$.
\textbf{Disagreement $|\Delta z|$.}
For each segment we $z$-score \FS{} and the human score within the analysis
corpus, then take $|z_{\mathrm{FS}}-z_{\mathrm{human}}|$.
Misses: top-1000 largest-$|\Delta z|$ segments.
Detects: top-100 smallest-$|\Delta z|$ segments (deprioritizing ceiling--ceiling).

(1)~Calque over-penalization;

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}
\begin{itemize}
\item Judge contamination risk: Gemini training data may overlap with the WMT25
  publication window (unquantified).
\item No new matched open-weight GEMBA+\FS{} historical reruns in this revision
  (budget). Section~\ref{sec:grid} provides matched Gemini contrasts on WMT25.
\item Fair-subset dropout on WMT25 is non-uniform (66.3\%--90.0\% by pair).
  On WMT25, chrF++ stays on its own subset ($n{=}4{,}200$; missing for EN$\to$it\_IT).
  For WMT22--24 MQM we recompute chrF++ (Section~\ref{sec:fair}, Experiment~\ref{sec:exp3}; $\rho{=}0.290$, PA${=}0.626$, $n{=}2{,}553$).
\item Model snapshot identity: headline Gemini run stored the API alias but not
  a pinned version string.
\item Proprietary prompt/weights limit full public reproduction
  (Appendix~\ref{app:schema} + confidential review access).
\item Error-analysis MQM subset ($n{=}824$) differs from correlation MQM subsets
  ($n{=}808$ / $2{,}628$) by mask construction.
\end{itemize}

\section{Discussion and conclusions}
We close by restating the claims the data support, the scope boundaries they do not cross, and the design implications for backbone choice.
\subsection{Main claims supported by the data}
On WMT25 ESA ($n{=}3{,}526$), \FS{}-Gemini reaches Spearman $0.613$, while matched
GEMBA-ESA on the same Gemini backbone is statistically tied on $\rho$ and
slightly ahead on PA ($0.730$ vs $0.722$).
The headline gap vs official GEMBA-ESA--GPT-4.1 is mostly backbone
(${+}0.058$ of ${+}0.065$); framework CI covers zero.
Gaps vs MetricX / XCOMET / CometKiwi / chrF++ by PA remain significant
($p{<}0.05$) on the primary mask.
On WMT22 MQM, \FS{}-GPT5 reaches $\rho{=}0.624$.

\subsection{Scope boundaries}
On extended WMT22--24 MQM, flat GPT-5 leads structured GPT-5 in $\rho$
($0.584$ vs $0.551$; $\Delta$PA n.s.).
Recomputed chrF++ on $n{=}2{,}553$ remains far below ($\rho{=}0.290$).
WMT25 advantages do not transfer uniformly to the full historical MQM cohort.
Error analysis: aggregate lead coexists with fluency-bias and register/dialect
blind spots.

\subsection{Backbone design}
The judge backbone materially affects MQM correlation.
Typology: \FS{}'s advantage over chrF++ correlates with syntactic distance; the
signal is weaker for neural metrics.
We argue for a pinned open-weight judge next.

\subsection{Conclusion}
\FS{} is a strong automatic fluency metric on WMT25 ESA under fair masks,
competitive on WMT22 MQM, and highly contingent on the judge backbone and
evaluation scope.
The honest reading of the Gemini-matched grid is that protocol gains are small
once the backbone is fixed; the broader WMT22--24 data favor
FluencyScore-MQM-flat-GPT5.
Error analysis makes behavior legible: calque/false-friend detects work;
fluent-but-wrong and dialect/register remain priority fixes.
A companion deployment system uses \FS{} as an online quality signal for Algebras
Agentic Localization (\url{https://algebras.ai}).
